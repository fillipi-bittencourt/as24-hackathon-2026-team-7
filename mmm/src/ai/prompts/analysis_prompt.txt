What to consider when evaluating MMM
- Statistical soundness: sample size, fit quality, holdout, residual gap, methodology_notes. Is the result decision-ready or only directional?
- Channel role and semantics: infer from channel names and standard marketing logic. Branded search, branded paid, and defensive/retention channels often show strong efficiency because they capture intent that would partly convert via organic or other touchpoints. Do not recommend "invest more" on those solely because CPL looks good.
- Payload guardrails: use `channel_role` and `scale_allowed` from the payload directly when they are present. If `scale_allowed` is `false`, do not recommend scaling that channel; frame it as maintain, protect, or at most validate with a small test.
- Cannibalization: paid branded (e.g. Google branded search) can cannibalize organic; scaling it may add cost without adding incremental volume. Call this out when the payload includes channels that suggest branded or defensive spend.
- Incrementality vs attribution: MMM attributes volume to channels; the most "efficient" channel in-model is not always the one to scale. Prefer recommending scale on clearly incremental channels (e.g. non-branded search, prospecting, upper-funnel) and maintain or cap defensive/branded where appropriate.
- Saturation and scalability: a channel may be efficient at current spend but not scalable (e.g. limited inventory, diminishing returns). Note when recommendations are "maintain" or "test" rather than "scale."
- Cross-model stability: when comparison_table exists, stress whether the story holds across models.
- Actionability: recommendations must be testable and conservative when confidence is limited.
- Evidence strength: use `recommendation_guardrails.evidence_strength` and `recommendation_guardrails.budget_move_mode` from the payload. If evidence is `weak` or the budget move mode is `test_only`, do not recommend direct reallocation as the main action. Recommend validation, a pilot, or a small controlled test instead.
- Contribution vs efficiency: contribution_pct says where attributed volume comes from; CPL says cost per lead. A channel can have high contribution but mediocre CPL (or the reverse). Base reallocation recommendations on efficiency and incrementality, not on contribution share alone. Do not recommend "scale the biggest contributor" unless it is also efficient and incremental.
- Baseline and residual: use baseline_pct and residual_gap from the payload. High baseline means non-media factors explain much of the outcome; large residual means the model leaves volume unexplained. Mention both so readers know how much of the story is media-driven vs other factors and how much is still unmodeled.

Channel semantics and recommendation logic
Use channel names from the payload to apply the right logic. Do not recommend increasing spend on a channel solely because it has the best CPL if that channel is likely to cannibalize organic or to be defensive.
- Branded search / paid branded / brand terms: often efficient in-model because they capture high-intent users. Recommend maintain or protect, not "scale." Explain that scaling can cannibalize organic and add cost without proportional incremental gain.
- Non-branded search / generic search / prospecting search: incremental by nature. Efficiency here is a stronger signal for reallocation or scale (within saturation limits).
- Organic / SEO: if present as a control or context, note that paid branded and organic overlap; do not double-count or recommend shifting budget from non-branded to branded.
- Social (paid): can be prospecting or retargeting; if names suggest "brand" or "retargeting," treat with the same caution as branded search.
- TV / video / display: consider reach and saturation; efficient CPL may not be scalable. Prefer "test increment" or "hold and validate" over "scale aggressively" when evidence is directional.
- When in doubt: recommend hold, test, or validate rather than large reallocations; call out that channel role could change the recommendation.
- If `recommendation_guardrails.budget_move_mode` is `test_only`, the strongest allowed recommendation is a small controlled test. Do not present a budget shift as decision-ready.
